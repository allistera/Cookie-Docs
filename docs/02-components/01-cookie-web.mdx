Cookie-Web is Cookie's browser client and the owner of the [shared Supabase schema](/data-model). The SPA is deployed at [mail.infinitywave.online](https://mail.infinitywave.online). Its browser-facing APIs run on the `cookie-web-*` Cloudflare Workers in Cookie-Worker; the only function left on Vercel is `/api/health`. The browser sends, schedules, cancels and sets follow-up reminders by calling the `cookie-web-send` Worker directly, posts composer attachments to that Worker's `POST /send/attachments` route and removes them with `DELETE /send/attachments/:id`, the same calls the iOS app makes.

## Draft retention

A draft that is not starred is deleted 30 days after its last edit. The deletion is permanent: there is no Trash for drafts to be restored from.

Each saved draft on the Drafts page has a star at the start of its row, and a line above the list states the rule. A starred draft is kept until it is sent or discarded. Starring does not count as an edit, so it does not reorder the list. Removing a star starts the 30 days again, so an old draft is never deleted the moment it loses its star. Drafts that already existed when the rule arrived have 30 days from the day it was applied.

The 30 days are fixed; there is no setting for them. Recoverable local drafts, which live only in the browser, cannot be starred and are not deleted by this rule. The star is offered in Cookie-Web only.

## Out of office

Open **Settings → Out of office**, choose an inclusive start/end date and an IANA
timezone, and review the subject and plain-text message in Preview. Check
**Enable automatic replies** and save to turn it on. It is off for every account
until explicitly enabled. Settings show disabled, scheduled, active, or expired;
the main app shows a banner while active. **End now** stops future dispatches. A
reply already handed to the provider may still arrive.

Only new mail received while that saved revision was enabled and within its
dated window can qualify. Editing or re-enabling starts a new revision; it never
revives old mail or changes an already queued reply's text. Cookie waits for
completed non-spam classification, excludes list/bulk mail, automated mail,
null-envelope senders, self-mail and screened/blocked mail, and uses a four-day
cooldown per normalized envelope sender. Delivery uses Cookie's configured
authorized sending address, never a From address entered in this form. Processing
can take several minutes. Incoming storage and original forwarding continue
independently.

The delivered mailbox address must also appear in To or Cc. Mail addressed only
through Bcc is excluded so an automatic reply does not reveal a hidden recipient.

**Replies needing review** appears when delivery could not be confirmed safely
or the provider rejected it. Check the provider's delivery history, then choose
**Verified delivered** or **Verified not delivered**. Neither action sends mail.
Uncertain replies pause further automatic replies to that sender until resolved.
Cookie stops retries before the provider's idempotency protection expires; it
does not promise exactly-once delivery after an ambiguous external failure.

Settings are private to the signed-in account. A conflicting save keeps the local
draft; **Discard edits and load latest** explicitly reloads the current revision.
The banner refreshes when the window gains focus and every minute.

## Sender blocking and new-sender screening

Sender decisions live in **Settings → Senders and screening**, which lists
accepted and blocked domains and addresses and lets you add a decision for an
address or a bare domain, or remove one. The saved list has a search box: it
narrows the rows already shown as you type and asks the Worker for the matching
page, so a decision beyond the first fifty is found too. Decisions apply to the sender's whole
domain and its subdomains: blocking `spam@badco.example` also blocks
`news@mail.badco.example`. Public email providers (gmail.com, outlook.com,
icloud.com and similar) are the exception; there a decision covers only that
exact address. Blocking affects future arrivals and already-held mail, which
move into **Blocked**; older ordinary mail stays in its current folders. The
reader offers no sender decisions for ordinary mail; held mail is the exception
described below.

**Screen unknown senders** is optional and off by default. A known sender is
only one whose domain (or, on a public provider, exact address) you explicitly
accepted. Previous correspondence, contacts and sent mail do not grant trust.
Matching trims surrounding spaces and ignores letter case; it does not collapse
plus aliases, remove dots or interpret wildcards. When decisions overlap, an
exact-address decision wins over its domain, and a subdomain's decision wins
over its parent domain's. Unusual addresses that cannot be accepted can still be
reviewed and restored one message at a time.

When screening is enabled, unknown arrivals wait in the sidebar's **New senders**
view. Opening a held message shows **Accept** and **Block** in the reader's
toolbar; after a decision the reader moves on to the next message awaiting
review. Selecting messages in the list offers the same, plus **Restore this
message only** for a single selection. Accept releases all held mail the decision covers and bypasses
screening for future arrivals. Mail from an accepted sender (its address or its domain)
opens with remote images loaded, as Important mail does; other mail keeps them
behind **Show images**, and held or blocked mail never loads them automatically.
Each earlier message in a conversation is judged on its own (Important, or from
an accepted sender), not by the message that was opened. Restore releases only the selected message and
does not make the sender known. Unblock removes the block; with screening enabled
the sender's blocked mail returns to New senders, otherwise it is released.
Removing acceptance affects future arrivals. Turning screening off leaves
already-held mail available for review.

Explicit blocking wins over screening. Acceptance bypasses screening, not spam
protection. Spam classification and label/category rules continue
independently; review views include held mail even when a rule archives it.
Release preserves the message's read, archive, spam and snooze state, so released
mail may belong in Done, Spam or Snoozed instead of Inbox. Held/blocked mail stays
out of ordinary folders, search, thread history and AI Today message items.
New senders retains held mail until you decide, however long that takes. Blocked
mail is deleted automatically on the retention set in **Settings → Spam** (30 days
by default, shared with the Spam folder): mail from an already-blocked sender is
deleted that many days after it arrives, and mail that moved into **Blocked** when
you blocked its sender is deleted that many days after the block, so nothing
disappears the moment you block someone. Unblocking or accepting the sender
before then keeps the mail. Once held or blocked mail is released, ordinary Spam
retention applies again using the original spam-verdict time.

Held/blocked arrivals do not produce browser or ntfy alerts or automatic replies.
Releasing them does not create delayed alerts or revive replies. Blocking also
suppresses pending alerts/replies to existing mail from that displayed From
address, even if its envelope sender differs. A browser alert or provider request
already handed off cannot be recalled. Blocking does not unsubscribe, reject
inbound delivery, change original forwarding, or restrict manual replies/sends.

## Tracking images

Senders put tracking images (open pixels) in mail so that the request for the
image tells them when the message was opened. Keeping remote images behind
**Show images** stops that for ordinary mail, but Important mail and mail from
accepted senders load their images at once, and pressing the button loads them
all. The reader therefore takes tracking images out of the message before it
is rendered, whatever the image rule says, and shows a notice above the message
saying how many it blocked. The Feed's posts do the same.

An image counts as a tracker when it is remote (an embedded or `cid:` image
makes no request) and either every size the sender declared for it is at most
one pixel (the `width` and `height` attributes, or inline `width`, `height`,
`max-width` and `max-height`), or its address uses one of the open-tracking
paths of the common mail platforms, for pixels that declare no size. The same
test covers every place a message can name an image: an image's `src` and
`srcset`, a `<picture>` source's `srcset`, the old `background` attribute and a
`url()` in an inline style. A tracking image or source is removed; a tracking
`background` attribute is removed and a tracking `url()` becomes `none`, so the
cell or block that carried it stays. A spacer
one pixel wide and twenty tall, an image with a percentage width, and a hidden
image a template shows again from a media query are all kept. The detection
runs in the browser on the sanitized body (`src/lib/trackingImages.js`); nothing
is stored, so an email whose only remote image was a tracker simply opens
without a **Show images** prompt.

## Mail list previews

Each row of a mail list shows the subject in bold, on read and unread mail
alike, and under it a preview of a sentence or two in regular weight. An unread
row is still marked by its dot, its bold sender and darker text. The preview is
written when the mail arrives, by the same classification that labels it (see
[AI features](/ai-features#2-classify-the-message)), and says what the subject
line leaves out rather than repeating it. It wraps to two lines at most.

Mail that has no written preview, which is sent mail and anything received
before previews were introduced, shows the start of its body in the same place.
Search results always show the start of the body. Drafts keep their one-line
rows.

## Paper Trail

**Paper Trail** sits under AI Today in the Email sidebar and lists receipts,
confirmations and other transactional mail: invoices, order, purchase, booking
and payment confirmations, refunds, shipping and delivery updates, and account
statements. It is a second place to find that mail, not a move: each message
stays in the Inbox, with its usual tab, read state and actions, and marking it
Done there leaves it listed in Paper Trail.

Membership is decided once, when the message arrives. The ingest classifier
answers whether the email is such a record (see
[AI features](/ai-features)) and, if so, attaches the system
**Paper Trail** label. The sidebar item opens that label's list
(`/inbox?filter=label&label=Paper Trail`), so it needs no route, table or
migration of its own. Being a system label it is not repeated in the sidebar's
Labels section or the tag menus; flagged rows wear a Paper Trail pill wherever
they are listed. Mail that arrived before the classifier learned the question
is not revisited, and confirmed spam is never filed.

## The Feed

**The Feed** sits under Paper Trail in the Email sidebar and shows the mail
carrying the **Newsletters** label that arrived today, by your device's
calendar day. It reads like a blog rather than a list: each newsletter is a
post, newest first, with its sender, time and subject as the heading and its
content open underneath, so the day's newsletters can be read in one scroll
without opening any of them. Mail you sent is left out, and The Feed is empty
when no newsletter has arrived yet or no Newsletters label is in use.

A newsletter is shown whether or not it has been marked Done. **Done** on a
post marks it done (and read) and leaves the post in place, wearing a Done
badge. The post's title opens it in the reader for everything else: reply,
labels, snooze, unsubscribe and attachments. Reading a post in The Feed does
not mark it read by itself; an unread newsletter keeps its dot until it is
opened or marked Done.

A post's content is the same sandboxed rendering the reader uses. Remote images
load straight away for Important mail and for senders you have accepted, and
sit behind **Show images** otherwise. Content is fetched a few posts ahead of
the part of the feed on screen instead of all at once, so a long day of
newsletters costs only what is read.

The Feed is a view, not a folder: it opens the Newsletters label's list with a
today flag (`/inbox?filter=label&label=Newsletters&received=today`) and narrows
the loaded rows in the browser, so it needs no route, table, migration or
Worker change of its own. The label list is newest first, so **Load more** only
appears while the oldest loaded row is itself from today. Newsletters is an
ordinary label, applied by hand, by a rule or by AI auto-tagging, and still has
its own entry in the sidebar's Labels section listing every day's mail as rows.

## Subscriptions

**Subscriptions** sits under The Feed in the Email sidebar and lists, in one
place, every sender whose mail offers a way to unsubscribe and that you have
not unsubscribed from yet: newsletters, mailing lists, marketing and product
updates. Each sender is one row with its name and address, how many of its
emails are in the mailbox, and the date of the newest one. The list is ordered
by that date, so the senders still writing to you are at the top.

**Unsubscribe** on a row asks that sender to stop, without opening any of its
mail. It is the same action as **Unsubscribe from sender** in the reader, and
works through the sender's newest email:

1. a one-click request to the sender, when it supports one;
2. otherwise an unsubscribe email sent from your address;
3. otherwise the AI tier works through the sender's unsubscribe page, which
   can take up to three minutes. The button reads **Unsubscribing…** meanwhile,
   and other senders can be unsubscribed from at the same time, up to ten a
   minute.

When the sender confirms, the row leaves the list and a toast says so. The
unsubscribe is remembered, so the sender stays off the list on the next visit
and on other devices, whether the unsubscribe was started here or in the
reader. When a sender cannot be unsubscribed from automatically (its page
wants a login or a CAPTCHA, for instance), the button becomes **Open
unsubscribe page**, a link to the sender's own page to finish there. Cookie
cannot tell whether that was completed, so such a row stays listed.

Unsubscribing changes nothing about the mail already received: it is not
marked Done, moved or deleted. Senders are allowed some days to act on a
request, so mail can still arrive for a while. A sender that writes again
after being unsubscribed from comes back onto the list, reading
**Unsubscribed** with the date and **still sending**, and offers
**Unsubscribe** again. When nothing is left to unsubscribe from, the page says
so.

A sender is detected from the `List-Unsubscribe` header its mail carries,
which bulk senders are required to include. Nothing is decided by AI, and
mail that arrived before this feature existed counts too. Left out are
senders whose only unsubscribe link is in the body of the email, mail in
Trash and Spam, mail you sent, and senders that are blocked or still waiting
under New senders. Senders are told apart by address, so a company that
writes from two addresses has two rows. The list holds the 1,000 senders with
the most recent mail.

Subscriptions is its own view at `/subscriptions`, fed by
`GET /messages/subscriptions` on `cookie-web-messages`.
[Cookie-Worker](/components/cookie-worker#subscriptions) documents the API
side, and [Operations](/operations#subscriptions) the order to deploy in.

## ntfy notifications

Settings → Notifications → **ntfy notifications** sends a push notification for
each new email through [ntfy](https://ntfy.sh). Enter the ntfy server (ntfy.sh
or a self-hosted server, as an `https://` address) and the topic you subscribe
to in the ntfy app, add an access token when the topic is reserved or the server
requires sign-in, and save. Saving turns delivery on; the switch pauses it with
the settings kept, and **Send test notification** publishes a test message at
once. **Generate** fills in a random, hard-to-guess topic.

The server must be a public `https://` host name: an IP address, a name without
a dot, or an internal name such as `.local` or `.internal` is refused, and a
server that answers a publish with a redirect has that publish counted as failed
rather than followed, so the token cannot be led elsewhere. The server's name is
also looked up on every publish, and a server whose name resolves to a private
address is refused: that publish fails as a network error would, and **Send test
notification** reports it as a failure. The rule is the one Cookie-Worker applies
to every [URL taken from mail or settings](/components/cookie-worker#requests-to-urls-from-mail-or-settings).
Topics follow ntfy's rule (1 to 64 letters, digits, hyphens or underscores), and
two Cookie accounts cannot share a topic on one server. The access token is sent
to the `cookie-web-notifications` Worker once, stored encrypted there, and never
shown again; Settings only shows its last four characters. When the Worker has
no `NTFY_TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, the token field is hidden and the topic must
accept anonymous publishing. Each alert carries the subject, a truncated
plain-text body and a link back to the email; held, blocked, muted and
category-silenced mail produces none, and muting a thread also drops its alerts
still waiting to be published. Migration `0110_ntfy_user_servers.sql`
adds the per-user server and token columns.

## Saved mail views

Create a view from the Mail tab of Search with **Save as view**, from the `/` command palette with **New saved view**, or with **+** beside the sidebar's Saved views heading. Name it, enter the search, and select an explicit folder: All mail, Inbox, Sent, Spam, Snoozed, or Done. If the search contains a supported `in:` folder, the form moves it into the folder selector. **Preview** runs the search in the form and lists the first few matching emails before you save. Saved views use keyword search and appear in the Email sidebar and the `/` command palette; the sidebar section only appears once there is at least one view. They load with the account in another browser.

Each sidebar view has a **⋯** menu. **Edit** opens the same form to change its name, search, or folder, and **Save as new view** keeps the original; an open saved view also has **Edit view** on its results page. **Delete** removes the definition at once and offers **Undo**, which restores it in its old position. **Manage** renames and reorders views. Each create, edit, or delete saves just that change: if another browser saved in the meantime, Cookie applies the change to the latest views and retries, and only stops when the same view was edited or deleted elsewhere, keeping your edit in the form.

Saved queries support words and one each of `from:` (or its `sender:` alias), `to:`, `tag:`, `has:attachment`, `before:YYYY-MM-DD`, and `after:YYYY-MM-DD`. Values with spaces can be quoted, for example `tag:"Client Project"`; text such as `in:inbox` inside a quoted tag value stays part of that value, not a folder choice. `from:` and `to:` are exact address matches. `before:` excludes its date; `after:` includes its date. Choose the folder separately rather than typing a top-level `in:` into the saved query. Boolean `AND`/`OR`/`NOT`, recognized filters with unsupported prefixes such as `-from:alice`, `is:starred`, unknown operators, duplicate filters (including `from:` plus `sender:`), and invalid dates are rejected with an explanation. Repeating sender filters does not create a sender group.

Each view opens `/search` with its query, `scope=mail`, `mode=keyword`, and a stable view ID. The results are fetched from the search Worker page by page, not from the inbox rows already loaded in the browser. Its Next button requires a verified live result beyond the current page; the shareable URL carries a bounded cursor and previous-page trail, so browser Back and Previous retain the correct page even when stale index hits are skipped. Search totals are estimates and do not control this Next button; the sidebar does not show counts. Verification scans at most the first 1,000 indexed hits. If that bound is reached without confirming an end, the page warns that later mail may exist rather than claiming there are no more results. Views are independent and may contain the same message. They do not affect Important/category tabs or move mail. All mail excludes deleted messages. Deleting a view deletes only its definition. A failed or conflicting save leaves the edit in the form for review.

The [saved-view API contract](/components/cookie-worker#saved-mail-views-api) describes the revision check and limits.

## Today's events in AI Today

AI Today opens with a **Today's Events** card above the suggested to-dos whenever any calendar has an event today; the classic Inbox lists mail only, keeping just the next-event chip beside its tabs. The card uses the same shape as the to-dos card: a header, then one row per event with its calendar's colour dot where the checkbox would be, the time (**All day** or start and end), the title, the calendar's name, and an **Open** button that opens the event in Calendar. All-day events come first, then the rest in start order, and events already over read quieter like done mail. The card and the next-event chip share one request for the day's events, refreshed every five minutes, on focus, and at midnight; if the calendar Worker is unreachable both simply disappear.

## Events over several days

The Calendar view's event dialog has an **End date** beside the start date, so an event can run overnight or across several days. Changing the start date moves the end date by the same number of days, and an end before the start is refused with "Choose an end after the start." For an all-day event the end date is the last day it covers, and editing only the title of a multi-day all-day event keeps all its days. A multi-day event, timed or all-day, is drawn on each day it covers, in the Calendar view and in the calendar sidebar.

## Calendar sidebar

Mail, Documents and Tasks can dock a read-only day calendar on the right. The calendar button at the right of the header or the `]` key (listed in the keyboard shortcuts dialog) shows and hides it; Calendar itself has no sidebar, being the full view. It starts closed, and whether it is open is remembered in that browser only, not across devices. The sidebar shows one day: a title with previous and next day controls and a Today button when another day is shown, a mini month for picking a day, a **Calendars** list, an **Up next** card for today's next timed event with a countdown, chips for all-day events, a 24-hour timeline that scrolls the current time into view with a now line, and an **Open Calendar** link to the same day. Overlapping events sit side by side and finished events fade. Every calendar the Calendar view lists is drawn, the **Tasks** one included: a task due that day sits among the events, as an all-day chip or at its due time for its estimate (thirty minutes without one), with a dashed edge and a checkbox glyph, and a completed one stays, ticked, faded and struck through. A task with a due time never becomes the Up next card. The Up next card has a **Join** button when the event's location or description holds an `https` link on a known video-call host (Google Meet, Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Whereby, Webex, FaceTime); other links never become a button. The **Calendars** list names every calendar the Calendar view lists, in the same order (Cookie calendars, subscriptions, Google Calendar) with **Tasks** last, each with its colour. Click one to hide its events and tasks from the day, Up next included, and again to show them; the choice lasts until the page is next loaded, when every calendar shows again, as in the Calendar view. The list folds away from its header, and being folded is remembered in that browser like the sidebar itself.

Clicking an event opens a details card with its time, calendar and location, the same Join button, and **Open in Calendar**, which opens that event in the Calendar view; Escape closes the card. A task's card shows whether it is done and has **Open task** instead, which opens it in Tasks. Nothing is created, edited or completed from the sidebar. While it is open it takes the place of the next-event chip beside the inbox tabs, and on a daily note it follows the note's date and replaces the daily-note calendar panel. It reads the existing `GET /calendars` route, `GET /calendar-events` from a week before the day, and the tasks worker's `GET /task-items?view=calendar&completed=1` window for the day and the one before it, so an event that runs past midnight or over several days, or a task that runs past midnight, shows its remainder from 00:00; if the tasks request fails the events still show. It refreshes every five minutes, on focus and when the tab becomes visible again, reads the tasks again as soon as one is changed elsewhere on the page, and moves to the new day at midnight unless a daily note pins it. Below 1100px wide it floats over the content instead of narrowing it, and on phones (760px and below) it is hidden and the chip stays.

## Tasks sidebar

Mail and Documents can dock a list of tasks on the right, in the place the calendar sidebar uses. The tasks button in the header, left of the calendar button, or the `[` key (listed in the keyboard shortcuts dialog) shows and hides it; Tasks has no sidebar, being the full view, and neither has Calendar. It starts closed, and whether it is open is remembered in that browser only, not across devices. One sidebar shows at a time: opening the tasks sidebar puts the calendar sidebar away, and the other way round. On a window narrower than 1100 px it floats over the content, and on a phone it is not offered.

The title names the day ("Tasks · Fri 27 Nov"). Under it are two sections, each with its count and a control to fold it; folded sections are remembered in that browser:

- **Today** lists every open task due today, from any project, in the Today list's order, with late tasks first. A late task says "Overdue" and its day in red; a task with a time shows the time. Each row names its project, or Inbox.
- **Inbox** lists the open tasks in the Inbox that are not already under Today. A task with a later day shows it; one on Someday shows no date.

A row's circle takes the task's priority colour, as in the Tasks view, and its labels follow as chips. Clicking the circle completes the task: the row leaves at once and comes back with a message if the tasks worker refuses. A repeating task moves on to its next date instead. Clicking the title opens the task in Tasks (`/tasks?project=today&task=<id>` or `project=inbox`). **Add task** under Today creates an Inbox task due today; under Inbox it creates one on Someday, as the Tasks view does. Enter or leaving the field saves, Escape drops the draft. An empty section says "Nothing due today." or "Your inbox is clear." **Open Tasks** at the foot goes to the Tasks app. Sub-tasks and dividers are left to the Tasks view, and renaming, dating, moving or deleting a task is done there.

The sidebar is `src/components/TasksRail.vue`, loaded as its own chunk, with its open state in `src/composables/useTasksRail.js` (localStorage `cookie.tasksRail.open`; the folded sections are `cookie.tasksRail.folded`) and its date wording in `src/lib/tasksRail.js`. It reads the first page of each list itself, `GET /task-items?project=today&date=<today>&view=page` and `GET /task-items?project=inbox&view=page`, so the list the Tasks view has loaded is not disturbed; a list longer than a page shows its count with a plus sign. Both are read again after any task is added, changed or deleted on the page, every five minutes, on coming back to the tab, and when the day changes. If one request fails that section says "Tasks could not be loaded." and the other still shows. No worker route was added.

## Calendar invitations

An email that carries a calendar invitation to the mailbox opens with an invitation card above the message: who invited, the event's title, its time on the reader's own clock, whether it repeats, and its location. **Accept**, **Maybe** and **Decline** sit under it. Opening the email tells the organiser nothing; pressing one of the three sends them a calendar reply, which their calendar reads as the guest's answer, and a copy lands in Sent under the invitation. The answer is the mailbox's, so it is the same on every device, and the card states it with the day the organiser was told. Pressing a different answer later tells the organiser again.

Accepting or answering maybe puts the event on Cookie's calendar, in the default calendar when that is one of Cookie's own, and **Go to event** opens it. Declining takes it off. An invitation holds one calendar entry however many times it is answered, updated or re-read.

When the organiser changes the event, the next email about it updates the same invitation and moves its calendar entry. If they changed the time, the card shows the new time and asks for an answer again; a change to the details alone keeps the answer. A cancellation shows the event as cancelled and removes it from the calendar. Opening an older email about the same event shows the latest details with a note that the message is an earlier version. The calendar changes when the email is read, not when it arrives.

Cookie sends mail as one address and can only answer for that address. An invitation sent to another of the person's addresses, such as the Gmail address their mail is forwarded from, is explained on the card and nothing is sent, since an answer from Cookie's address would not count for that guest; the plain **Add to calendar** offer stays for those. An invitation to a single date of a repeating event is answered on its own, and is added to the calendar only when the series is not already there. A repeating event with a pattern the calendar cannot express (every second week, the last Friday) is added as its first date only.

The card is `src/components/InvitationCard.vue`, loaded as its own chunk when an invitation is open, with its wording in `src/lib/calendarInvitation.js`. It reads `calendar_invitation` from `GET /messages/calendar-invite` and answers through `POST /send/invitation-reply`; [Cookie-Worker](/components/cookie-worker) documents both.

## Writing suggestions

While you write a message, Cookie suggests the words most likely to come next. They appear in grey after the caret, followed by a small **Tab** marker.

- **Tab** writes the suggestion into the message and leaves the caret after it. Clicking or tapping the grey words does the same. Undo takes it back out.
- **Keep typing** to ignore it. If what you type matches the start of the suggestion, the rest stays on show; anything else puts it away and a new one is offered at the next pause.
- **Escape** puts the suggestion away without closing the composer. In the reply box it never closes the reader while you type, with or without a suggestion. Moving the caret, clicking elsewhere or leaving the editor also puts it away.

A suggestion is offered after a pause of about 0.4 seconds, and only when the caret is at the end of a line with at least a few characters written, so nothing appears while you edit the middle of a sentence or type in a code block. It is offered in the compose window and in the reply box, where the email being answered is used as context. The signature and snippet editors in Settings never ask for suggestions. While the slash-command menu is open, the suggestion stays out of the way and Tab keeps its usual meaning; when no suggestion is on show, Tab moves to the next control as before.

Suggested words are not part of the message until they are accepted: they are never saved in a draft and never sent.

Settings → Signature → **Writing suggestions** switches the feature off. The choice is kept in that browser (`localStorage` key `cookie.smartCompose.off`), so it is set per device. While it is on, the text before the caret is sent to the AI model at each pause; see [AI features](/ai-features#writing-suggestions) for the request, its limits and its cost.

The behaviour lives in `src/lib/smartCompose.js`, which `ComposerEditor.vue` loads as its own chunk when it is given a `smartCompose` prop (the subject, recipients and reply message), so the code stays out of the inbox view's bundle budget. The suggestion is an empty `<span class="composer-ghost">` placed after the caret whose words are drawn by CSS from its `data-suggestion` attribute. They are therefore never in the editor's text and cannot be selected or typed into, and the editor removes the span before it reads its own HTML, which is the one place a draft's content comes from. Tab inserts the words with the browser's `insertText` command so the insertion joins the undo history. A failed or refused request shows nothing and stops further requests for a minute. Session Replay masks the attribute, as it masks text.

## Cc and Bcc

A message can carry Cc and Bcc recipients as well as the people it is written to.

- **In the composer.** `+ cc` and `+ bcc` appear under the title while the subject or a recipient field has the focus, which it does when the window opens. Pressing one adds that line under the "to" field and puts the cursor in it. Each line takes addresses separated by commas and suggests contacts as the "to" field does. The cross at the end of a line removes it. Once the message itself is being written the two links go away, and so does a line that was added and left empty; a line that holds an address stays on show.
- **In the reply box.** The same two links sit at the right of the "Reply to" heading. **Reply all** writes to the sender and the original "to" list and keeps the original Cc list on a Cc line, where it can be changed before sending. Switching between Reply and Reply all resets the Cc line to match; a Bcc is kept.

To, Cc and Bcc share one limit of 20 recipients, and Send stays off while any entry is not an address. Tab runs subject, to, Cc, Bcc, then the message, skipping lines that are not shown. The links are reached with the pointer or by touch; the Tab order does not stop on them.

Drafts, Send Later and an undone send keep all three lines. The Sent copy stores them too, including Bcc, which the delivered mail never shows. The Drafts and Scheduled lists still name only the "to" recipients.

The lines are `src/components/ComposerCopies.vue`, shared by `ComposerWindow.vue` and the reply box in `TraditionalInboxView.vue` (which loads it as its own chunk). The browser sends `cc` and `bcc` to `POST /send` on `cookie-web-send` only when someone is on them. [Cookie-Worker](/components/cookie-worker#cc-and-bcc-recipients) documents the API side, and [Operations](/operations#cc-and-bcc) the order to deploy in.

## Several messages and emails open at once

Only one message fits the composer and one email the reader, so the others wait as tabs along the bottom right of the screen, each titled by its subject (a message with no subject yet reads "New message"). Clicking a tab brings it back; clicking the tab of whatever is open puts it away again, and the close button on a tab removes it.

- **Messages.** The composer has a minimise button beside its close button. Pressing **Compose** (or running the Compose command) while a message is being written keeps that message as a tab and starts a new one; the same happens when a reply or forward is opened in the composer, a saved draft is opened from Drafts, or an undone or failed send returns to the composer. A message nothing was written in is closed instead of kept. Minimising saves the message exactly as closing the composer does, so it is in Drafts as well; opening that draft from Drafts brings its tab back rather than a second copy, and discarding it there removes the tab. Opening a draft does not save it again, so it keeps its place in Drafts until something in it changes. Closing a tab closes the message and leaves it in Drafts. The AI draft side panel is not kept with a minimised message.
- **Emails.** The reader has a minimise button at its top left that closes the reader and keeps the email as a tab. Its tab opens the email again in the folder it was read in. Closing the tab only removes the tab. A tab goes when its email is marked Done, deleted, snoozed or moved to or from Spam, since it has left that folder.

A lone message or email that is already open has no tab under it; the tabs appear once there is something to switch to. The composer sits on top of them.

Tabs come back after a page refresh. They are stored in that browser's `localStorage`, one list per account (`cookie.dockTabs.<account>`), and restored if the page last held them within the hour; after that they are dropped, and minimised messages remain in Drafts. Only what brings a tab back is stored, never a message itself: an email tab keeps its id, subject, sender and folder, and a message tab keeps the id of its row in Drafts and its subject. A restored message tab opens that draft, so a message that has no row yet (nothing written in it, or a save that had not reached the server) is not restored, and a tab whose draft has since been sent or discarded elsewhere is removed once Drafts has loaded. The message that was open in the composer at the refresh comes back as a tab too. Tabs are not synced between browsers or devices.

## Synced composer preferences

Settings > Email signature and Settings > Compose snippets save to the authenticated Cookie account. A new browser loads them when the user signs in; the signature fills new messages, and saved snippet triggers appear in the composer slash menu. Edits are committed only after the server accepts a save. If another session saved first, Cookie displays the latest account values and keeps the local draft for review.

Snippets can include `{{recipient.first_name}}`, `{{recipient.name}}`, and `{{recipient.email}}`, plus editable fields such as `{{fill:meeting time}}`. Type a saved slash trigger in a new message or inline reply to preview it, review suggested recipient values, and fill the fields before inserting. Cookie uses a recipient name only when it matches one selected address; if the name is unavailable or there are several recipients, enter the value yourself. Values are inserted as text, not HTML. Unfilled fields remain visible in the draft, and Send, Send Later, and inline reply Send warn and stop until those fields are filled or removed. Other brace text and older snippets remain literal and continue to work.

This expansion and send check currently belongs to Cookie-Web. Clients that consume the shared `id`/`name`/`html` snippet records, including a future mobile snippets UI, need equivalent expansion and unresolved-field checks before sending.

Older browser-local signature and snippet values are never assigned to an account automatically. If this browser has them, Settings previews the old values and offers checkboxes to replace the account's signature, snippets, or both. Only values chosen in a successful import are removed from the old browser keys. Signing out clears the active in-memory composer preferences and draft; there is no account-scoped browser cache for these preferences. Before it removes the device's offline data, logging out tries once more to send changes made offline, and asks before discarding any still unsent or any drafts saved only on the device.

The cross-client API contract is documented in [Cookie-Worker's composer preferences section](/components/cookie-worker#composer-preferences-api). Mobile clients can reuse it without adopting Cookie-Web's Settings UI.

## Share availability from a draft

Choose **Share availability** below the editor in a new message or inline reply. Select a date range of at most 31 days, a meeting duration, working hours, a proposal timezone, and up to ten calendars. All days in the selected range are considered. Cookie shows up to 100 non-overlapping suggestions; select up to ten, choose **Review selected times**, edit the proposal text, and insert it into the existing draft. The inserted text includes the IANA timezone, UTC offsets, and duration. It remains editable and explicitly labels the times as proposals. Inserting them does not create an event, reserve a time, send an invitation, or produce a booking link.

The **Calendar interpretation timezone** is separate from the proposal/working-hours timezone. Cookie's stored event times have no original timezone, and date-only or floating subscription events also need an interpretation. Confirm that zone before checking. Subscribed timed events with an explicit supported timezone retain their source instants. Choose calendars whose floating times can share the confirmed interpretation zone.

Every check reads owned Cookie events and fetches the selected subscriptions afresh for the requested range; it never calculates free time from cached subscription rows alone. Calendar checkboxes show the last cached sync time, whether it is over 24 hours old or failed, and whether the selected range was freshly checked. A fresh availability check does not update the cached calendar or its last-sync timestamp. A failed, excessive, ambiguous, or unsupported calendar check blocks suggestions until the range or calendar selection changes or a retry succeeds. [Availability API limits](/components/cookie-worker#availability-read-api) describe the supported feed forms.

All-day, recurring, overnight, and overlapping events block their busy intervals. Suggestions exclude nonexistent and repeated daylight-saving wall times and meetings that would cross an offset change. Availability can change after the check; the review shows its timestamp, and recipients still need confirmation. Event titles and subscription URLs are never inserted. Signing out or changing accounts clears the shared calendar cache, closes availability review, and ignores outstanding responses from the previous account.

## Tasks and unified search

Cookie's Tasks app stores user-authored work separately from the overnight enricher's email-derived `tasks` rows. It supports a static Inbox, Today (including overdue work), nested projects, editable task details, completion, reassignment, due dates, Todoist-style priorities (P1 most urgent through P4 default, shown as coloured flags in the panel and coloured completion circles in the list), and subtasks through the task panel.

The task panel opens over the list at `/tasks?task=<id>`. Its header holds the task's project as a chip; click it for a list with a search box (**Type a project name**), the Inbox, then **My Projects** with every project indented under its parent, the current one ticked. Typing narrows the list, the arrow keys and Enter choose, Escape closes it, and choosing a row moves the task. Beside it are up and down arrows that step through the tasks beside it in the list, **Copy link to task**, an expand toggle that fills the window, a **More actions** menu (**Duplicate**, **Move to…**, **Copy link to task**, **Copy as text**, **View activity**, **Open in new window** and **Delete task**, which asks first) and close. Click the title to rename it. The description is plain text read as a small markdown: `#` headings, paragraphs, `-` and `1.` lists, `>` quotes, fenced code, and `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `~~strikethrough~~`, `` `code` `` and `[links](https://…)` inline. Read-only it is rendered; click it or press **Format** to edit it in a box with a toolbar (H1, bold, italic, strikethrough, link, code, bulleted and numbered lists, quote) that writes those marks around the selection, and press **Done** or leave the field to save (Escape discards). Under it, **Sub-tasks** shows a done count and progress bar, a **Sort** choice (manual, name, due date or priority, for the panel only), **Hide completed**, and an arrow on each row that opens that sub-task in the panel; **Add sub-task** adds one. **Comments** and **Activity** are tabs at the foot of the panel; Activity is a plain list of the changes made to this task (created, renamed, moved, due date set, completed, and so on) with when each happened, newest first, and **Show more** for older ones; changing its place in a list is not recorded. Account-wide history and completion goals stay under **History** on the task list. The right-hand rail groups **Organise** (labels), **Priority** (the four levels in a row, the current one pressed) and **Schedule**, one chip that reads the date (Today, Tomorrow, Sat 24 Oct, Someday, or No date) and the time. Click it for a scheduler like Todoist's: a box to type a date (**tomorrow**, **24 Oct**, **2026-10-24**, **someday**, or blank for none, then Enter), **Tomorrow**, **Next week** (Monday), **Next weekend** (Saturday), **Someday** and **No date** shortcuts, a scrolling calendar from this month a year on with arrows and a return-to-this-month button, and **Time** (with its time zone, once there is a date) and **Repeat** (the repeat rule and **Save repeat**) at the foot. Choosing a date closes it; Escape closes it without leaving the panel. **Someday** puts a task off with no day in mind: it takes no time or repeat (choosing it drops both), is never due or overdue, sits on no day of the calendar, and sorts after every dated task. It is stored as the due date `9999-12-31`, the last date the tasks API accepts, so other clients of the API (the MCP server included) see that date; a filter's "any date" rule matches it and "no date" does not. Someday is where a new task starts: one added from **Add task** under a list, from the **Add Task** form when its text names no day, or by **AI Task** is put off to Someday rather than left with no date. The add-task form's full view has a **Someday** tick box beside the due date, ticked to begin with; enter a due date to give the task that day instead, or untick the box to add it with no date. Sub-tasks, whether added in the panel or generated by AI Task, get no date of their own, **Duplicate** copies the date of the task it copies, and tasks that arrive from Todoist or through the API keep whatever date they were sent with. The rail ends with when the task was added and by whom. A task with a due date also appears on Cookie Calendar on that day (an all-day item, or a thirty-minute slot at its due time), under a "Tasks" toggle in the calendar sidebar; clicking it opens the task panel over the calendar, without leaving it or changing the address, and what the panel saves (a new date, a rename, completion, a delete) is read back onto the calendar straight away. A completed task stays on its day rather than leaving the calendar: its box is ticked and its title struck through and faded. In the day view the box on a task's chip is a checkbox of its own: click it to complete the task where it sits, or again to reopen it, without opening the panel; only the rest of the chip opens the panel. If the save fails the tick is undone. The calendar reads them from the tasks worker's `GET /task-items?view=calendar&from=&to=&completed=1` window (at most 100 days). Tasks put off to Someday line a **Someday** row along the foot of the calendar in every view, under the same Tasks toggle and read through the same endpoint with `from` and `to` both `9999-12-31`. The row is shown only while there is a Someday task to put in it, and tasks are not added from it: a new task lands there on its own, and an existing one is put off to Someday from the task list or its panel. Tick a task's circle to complete it (it stays in the row, ticked, until the calendar is next opened, so the tick can be taken back), or click its title to open it in the task panel over the calendar. Drag a task's title onto the day lane or a week column to give it that day and time: a dashed slot follows the pointer in quarter hours, as long as the task's estimate (thirty minutes without one), and dropping it saves the due date, time and the browser's time zone, moves the task onto the day and takes it out of the row; if the save fails it returns to its place. The month view takes no drops, having no times. The day view lays the whole day out left to right as one strip, midnight to midnight, wider than the window: it opens on 8 AM to 7 PM, and a sideways swipe (on a touch screen or trackpad), a sideways or Shift wheel, or the scrollbar under it carries it through the earlier and later hours. Moving to another day keeps the hours in view; switching to the week or month view and back returns to 8 AM, and **Show** on a scheduling conflict outside those hours scrolls to it. The current-time line is drawn at any hour of today. The week view still shows 8 AM to 7 PM. In the day view, a break of an hour or more between two events is named in light text at the foot of the lane (**2hrs**, **1hr 30m**); timed tasks count as events here, and the time before the first event or after the last is not labelled. An event or timed task in the day lane can be dragged along it to another time that day: its chip dims, a slot in its colour follows the pointer in quarter hours and names the new time, and letting go saves the new start (for a task, its due time) without changing its length; if the save fails it goes back. The slot stays inside the day, and a press that does not travel still opens the event. A repeating Cookie event does not drag, since that would move every occurrence (change it in its dialog), and nor do events on a subscribed or read-only calendar or the next-day remainder of an event or task running past midnight. A timed event that runs past midnight is drawn on every day it covers, in Calendar's day, week and month views, the calendar sidebar and the daily-note calendar panel: the pieces after its first day start at 00:00 and open the whole event, and only the first day's piece can be dragged. Saving such an event from its dialog without changing its start or end time keeps its length, since the end field cannot show a time on a later day.

Labels are managed from a Labels section in the Tasks sidebar and from Settings > Tasks > Task Labels: each has a colour and shows how many tasks carry it, click one in the sidebar to list its tasks across projects, drop a task onto it to tag the task, and rename or delete it everywhere at once. Tasks take labels from a chip picker that suggests existing ones; a new name typed as `@name` is registered with the default colour.

Saved filters sit in a Filters section of the Tasks sidebar, between Today and My Projects. A filter is a named set of up to ten rules on due date (overdue, today, tomorrow, next 7 days, any date, no date), project (including Inbox), priority, and label, matched as all or any, with any rule flipped to "is not". It lists the open tasks it fits across every project, by due date with undated tasks last, and names each task's project. Rename it in place, edit or delete it from the row (delete offers Undo), or change its rules from **Edit filter** under the title. Filters are one revisioned document per account in `users.prefs.savedTaskFilters`, read and replaced through `cookie-web-tasks`'s `GET/PUT /task-filters`; the list comes from `GET /task-items?view=page&project=filter:<id>&date=` and is evaluated in SQL.

Each list keeps the order you give it. In the list layout every row has a grip at its edge: drag it onto the upper half of another row to place it before that row, or the lower half to place it after, and the new order is saved at once (if the server refuses, the rows slide back with a notice). With the grip focused, Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the row one place. The Inbox, each project and each label list has an order of its own, and new tasks join at the bottom. Today is ordered by due date, with each day arranged separately in an order that belongs to Today alone, so re-arranging a day there never moves a task among its siblings in its project. A saved filter's list and the board layout cannot be re-arranged. Dragging a row onto the sidebar instead moves it: onto a project or the Inbox to file it there, onto Today to make it due today, or onto a label to tag it.

Dividers are rules dropped between rows to group them; each can carry a short heading centred on the rule, edited in place. A divider moves like any row but is never placed next to another divider.

Several tasks can be edited at once. Shift-click a task (or press Shift+Enter or Shift+Space on it) to start a selection, after which plain clicks add or remove tasks, or choose **Select all** above the list to take every task on the page (dividers are never selected). While anything is selected a bar floats over the list, like the inbox's bar over checked emails, with the count, **Select all** when the page is only partly selected, **Done**, **Move** (the Inbox and every project except the list on screen), **Reschedule** (today, tomorrow, next week, someday, or no date), **Priority**, **Label** (adds a label to the tasks that lack it) and **Delete** (asks first; sub-tasks go with their parents). Each edit is applied task by task and reported once, naming how many it reached if the server refused any. The selection can also be dragged onto the sidebar's Inbox, Today, a project, or a label. Escape closes an open menu, then clears the selection.

Each task carries a discussion thread, shown on the **Comments** tab of the task panel with its count. Entries read oldest first and hold text, one attached file, or both: write in the composer at the foot (Enter sends, Shift+Enter makes a newline), choose a file with the attach button (any type up to 25 MB; the chosen file shows as a chip that can be removed before sending), add an emoji from the picker, and press the send button. Each entry shows the author's avatar, name and time and is marked "(edited)" once changed. On your own entries, hover for **Edit** (in place; Enter saves, Escape cancels) and **Delete** (asks first); an entry that keeps its text can also have its attachment removed from the chip's close button, while an entry that is only its attachment is deleted whole. Clicking an attachment opens images and PDFs in a new tab and downloads anything else. Threads come from `cookie-web-tasks`'s `/task-comments` routes and stay loaded while the Tasks app is open, so reopening a task does not refetch; a deleted task takes its thread with it.

Settings > Tasks > Todoist mirrors a Todoist account into Tasks. Paste the Todoist API token (Todoist: Settings > Integrations > Developer); `cookie-web-tasks` checks it with Todoist, stores it encrypted and shows only its last four characters. Todoist stays the source of truth: its projects become projects (the Todoist Inbox is Cookie's Inbox), its sections become dividers, and its open tasks arrive with their sub-tasks, labels, priority, due date and time, and repeat rule when Cookie can phrase it. The sync runs on the hour from 07:00 to 20:00 in the Todoist account's time zone while **Sync every hour** is on, and **Sync now** runs it at once and refreshes the Tasks view. **Sync projects** and **Sync tasks** choose what a run mirrors: with projects off, the projects already here stay as they are and incoming tasks are filed into them, or into the Inbox when their project is not here; with tasks off, the tasks already here are left alone. A task whose Todoist project is unknown here always lands in the Inbox. The page shows the last run's time, counts (projects, sections, open tasks, completions, removals, skipped, and completions sent to Todoist) or failure. Tasks completed or deleted in Todoist are completed or removed here. Completing a mirrored task in Cookie completes it in Todoist as well, normally within a second, and marking it not done again reopens it there; completing a repeating task moves it to its next occurrence in both. If Todoist cannot be reached, the change is sent by the next sync. Other edits made in Cookie to a mirrored task are overwritten by the next run, while tasks added in Cookie are never touched. Disconnecting forgets the token and keeps the mirrored work; saving a token for a different Todoist account first turns the previous account's mirrors into ordinary Cookie rows.

The header search is available across applications and routes to `/search`. `cookie-web-search` federates Meilisearch results from the `messages`, `documents`, and `tasks` indexes, then hydrates owned rows from Postgres. Scope tabs can restrict results to mail, documents, or tasks. The **AI chat** button beside the header's calendar button opens the Cookie Assistant drawer on any app: a conversation over mail, documents and tasks served by Meilisearch's conversational search through `cookie-web-search`'s `POST /chat` (see [AI features](/ai-features)).

## Not important

When classification puts an email in **Important** (a high priority, or a category named Important), the reader's **More** menu offers **Not important**. It lowers that email's priority to normal, clears a category named Important, and remembers the sender in `sender_importance_feedback` (migration `0087`). From then on `mail-app-ingest` never rates that sender's mail high priority and leaves categories named Important out of the choices it gives the classifier, so the model files the mail under the best other category. The confirmation toast offers **Undo**, which forgets the sender and restores the email's previous priority and category. Mail that is Important only because it is due now is unaffected.

## Documents folder browser and uploaded files

The Documents dashboard is a folder browser: it shows one folder at a time, subfolders first, then documents and uploaded files together by name, as cards or rows (the choice is remembered per browser). A breadcrumb leads back up, and the sidebar tree follows the browser: opening a folder, or a document inside one, expands that folder and its parents so the current location is always visible. Starred, tag, and search views keep the flat table because they cut across folders.

### What can be uploaded

- **Any file type.** There is no allow-list; the only limit is **25 MB per file**. Larger files are refused with `413` before any bytes are stored, and a user may start at most 60 uploads per minute (`429` beyond that).
- **Upload** in the toolbar adds files to the folder currently shown.
- **Drag files in from the desktop** and drop them anywhere on the Documents dashboard, its header and the starred, tag, and search lists included, to add them to the folder currently shown (the root when none is open). The dashboard is outlined while files are dragged over it. Drop them onto a folder card, or onto a folder in the sidebar tree, to add them to that folder instead; the sidebar takes drops while a document is open too. A toast names the folder the files went to, or says how many could not be uploaded. Folders cannot be uploaded: one dragged in is skipped with a message, so drop the files inside it instead. Files dropped into an open document belong to the editor, which takes images.
- Uploaded files and documents can be dragged onto a folder card or a sidebar folder to move them, or moved later from the item menu.
- The file's type is detected from its first bytes for JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, and PDF. Anything else keeps the type the browser declared, or `application/octet-stream` when it declares none. The stored name is trimmed to 255 characters.

### Where files live

| Piece | Location |
| --- | --- |
| Bytes | Private Cloudflare R2 bucket `cookie-files`, bound to `cookie-web-tasks` as `FILES`, keyed by `<user_id>/<uuid>` so a key never carries a client-supplied name |
| Metadata | `document_files` in Supabase (name, MIME type, size, folder, owner), migration `0076` |
| Access | Only through the authenticated `cookie-web-tasks` Worker `/files` routes; the bucket is never public and every read checks ownership first |

Files are not indexed for search, cannot be tagged, and are never read by Document AI. Deleting a folder moves its files (like its documents) to the root instead of destroying them; deleting a file removes the metadata row first and then the R2 object.

### Opening and downloading

Image files show a thumbnail of their bytes in place of the icon, in both the grid and the list, fetched through the same authenticated route once the card scrolls into view and cached per file for the session. Images and PDFs, the sniffed types only, open in an inline preview at `/documents/file/<id>`, with a download button. Every other type downloads with its original name. The Worker serves both with `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, so a file uploaded with a misleading declared type is never rendered as something it is not.

### Email attachments

**Email attachments** is a built-in folder, pinned first in the root of Documents and linked at the top of the sidebar's Documents tree. It lists every file the mailbox received or sent by email, so a file can be found without remembering which email carried it.

- **Organised by month.** Files are listed newest first under a heading per month (**November 2026**). Each row has the file's name, who it came **From** or went **To** (with a count of the other recipients), the subject of its email, its kind, size and day.
- **All, Received, Sent.** The control in the toolbar keeps one side of the mailbox. In **All**, each row says whether it was received or sent.
- **Pictures from email bodies** (logos, signatures, screenshots placed in the text) are left out, because they are most of what mail carries. The **Show pictures from email bodies** checkbox lists them too, marked **Picture in email**.
- **Download** a file from its name or its download button. A file whose contents were not stored at delivery is listed with the button disabled.
- **Open email** (the envelope button) opens the email the file came with in the reader.
- A hundred files load at a time; **Older attachments** adds the next hundred below.

The folder is a view of the mailbox, not a place files are kept. Nothing is copied into Documents, so its files cannot be renamed, moved, deleted, tagged or dropped into, and they do not count as uploaded files. A file leaves the list when its email goes to Trash, and files on spam and on mail from held or blocked senders are never listed. The list is not searchable yet, and it is not on iOS.

[Cookie-Worker](/components/cookie-worker#attachment-library) documents the API behind it, and [Operations](/operations#email-attachments) the order to deploy in.

## Document editing and bounded lists

**Table of contents** in the "/" menu inserts a block that lists the document's headings, indented by level. It stores no content of its own: the list updates as headings change, and choosing an entry moves the caret to that heading. Markdown and HTML exports rebuild it from the headings, with anchor links.

Hovering a heading shows a toggle to the left of **+**. **>** collapses everything under that heading up to the next heading of the same or higher level, and a down arrow expands it again. Collapsed sections are remembered per document in the browser's `localStorage`; they are a view preference only, so the blocks are still saved, exported, and searched. Typing into a hidden block, or jumping to one from the table of contents, expands its section.

Pasting code into a paragraph creates a code block instead of reformatted text. Cookie recognises a multi-line copy from VS Code (using its language mode), a Markdown code fence, a lone `<pre>` from a web page, and monospace pre-formatted HTML from other IDEs. The language comes from the mode, `language-*` style classes, or the fence info, mapped onto the code block's supported languages; anything else becomes plain text. A code block pasted into an empty paragraph replaces it; otherwise it is added after the paragraph.

The document icon picker opens on **Icons**, a built-in set of about 250 Material Symbols icons in themed groups with search, and keeps an **Emoji** tab for the previous emoji picker. Built-in icons render from the self-hosted icon font rather than the operating system's emoji font, so they look the same everywhere. They are stored in the existing `emoji` field as `ms:<icon_name>`; `cookie-web-tasks` accepts `ms:` followed by up to 64 lowercase letters, digits, or underscores, and Cookie-iOS shows them as the closest SF Symbol, falling back to a document symbol.

Plain tables use a lightweight editable grid with row and column controls; **Open spreadsheet** activates formulas and advanced tools. Existing formula tables and workbook snapshots keep the full spreadsheet editor. Offscreen rich editors retain their stored data until activation, and document blocks become interactive only after Editor.js has installed its change observer.

Before the editor shows a document, the text of its paragraphs, headings and list items goes through the same inline allow-list as the export, so HTML that another writer stored (through the API or the MCP server) is not rendered as it is. The Worker applies the same allow-list when blocks are saved, by any writer, and removes other markup rather than refusing the save (see [Cookie-Worker](/components/cookie-worker#document-block-html-cookie-web-tasks)); the browser check covers blocks stored before that. Hiding or closing the page sends a pending save at once rather than waiting for the autosave timer. Starring, moving or changing the icon of the open document does not make its next save report a conflict, and a document renamed in the folder browser shows its new name at once.

Large mail and document lists render a bounded window, locating visible rows with binary search. Document/task pages are fetched incrementally. Task deep links load the full task before deciding whether it exists; network failures remain retryable. Creating an AI document completes its save without overriding navigation made while creation was pending.

## Configuration highlights

| Variable | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `DATABASE_URL` | Supabase Postgres connection for the health check and local tooling |
| `VITE_AUTH0_DOMAIN` / `VITE_AUTH0_CLIENT_ID` / `VITE_AUTH0_AUDIENCE` | Browser Auth0 configuration |
| `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` / `VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` | Content-free Realtime inbox pings |

The browser calls the send Worker origin directly with the user's bearer token, refuses redirects, and gives up after 25 seconds: a send with no answer in that time, or one lost to a network failure, is reported as `DELIVERY_PENDING` and its request ID is kept, because the email may have gone. Vercel preview deployments and local development against the production send Worker are refused by its CORS allowlist, as they are by every other Worker. Delivery credentials, quotas, reminder validation, tracking pixels, and sent-copy persistence are owned by Cookie-Worker. A confirmed send releases its request ID so identical new mail can be sent intentionally. A failure follows the [send error contract](/components/cookie-worker#send-error-contract), which `src/lib/mailSending.js` applies: a failure where the email cannot have gone, such as a provider rejection (`422` `DELIVERY_REJECTED`), a validation error or a quota refusal, releases the ID, and the composer says "The email was not sent. Check it, then send it again." so the draft can be edited first. `DELIVERY_PENDING`, `DELIVERY_NOT_SENT` and `5xx` failures keep the ID and the retry resumes the same delivery; `DELIVERY_UNCERTAIN` and `DELIVERY_CONFLICT` keep it and are shown without a retry.

`/api/health` checks every Worker's `/health` route, Postgres and Meilisearch. One run's result answers every request to that function instance for the next 30 seconds.

The Worker API origins are public constants in `src/lib/apiWorkers.js`, not environment secrets.

Pushes to `main` run format, lint, unit, build, and Chromium end-to-end jobs. Vercel deploys the SPA automatically. The schema's migrations live in Cookie-Worker and are applied from there; see the [data model](/data-model).
